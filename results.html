Case 01 — Moving, Toronto

Advance Moving

Two trucks in October 2024. Eight today, 26 months in.

Trucks at the start2
Trucks now8
Booked revenue through the account$5M+
Period26 months

The situation

Two trucks, a good local reputation, and an agency running one broad-match campaign with a Performance Max campaign on top. Long distance and local moves shared the same budget.

What was actually broken

Performance Max was 61% of spend, and most of its reported conversions were phone clicks on the careers page. Broad match was buying searches for junk removal and storage units.

What we changed

Performance Max off on day one. Four campaigns: local, long distance, packing and materials, storage. Their own name as exact match for pennies. Call recording on every number.

What else was happening

They put a third truck on in April and hired an office manager who answers from 7am. Both matter. We would put the account at roughly two thirds of the change.

What we would do differently

We left the old Performance Max campaign running three weeks alongside the rebuild to be safe. It cost about $1,900 and taught us nothing.

Case 02 — Restoration, four trucks

Cakewalk Restore

Water, fire and smoke work, run off the phone.

Booked jobs a month, baseline14
Booked jobs a month, month 626
Calls answered 6pm–7am59% → 94%
PeriodSep 2025 – Feb 2026

The situation

Four trucks, decent adjuster relationships, and an account left on autopilot for eleven months by the previous agency.

What was actually broken

The phone. In month one we changed nothing except turning call recording on. 41% of calls between 6pm and 7am went to voicemail, and those are the twenty-thousand-dollar jobs.

What we changed

Showed the owner the recordings. He put an answering service on at $310 a month. Then bids followed the new answer rate, with emergency water and fire split out from planned work.

What else was happening

A wet December, and a storm week in mid-January that put nine jobs in the diary in five days. Take that week out and month 6 is 21 jobs, not 26.

What we would do differently

Nothing in the account, but we should have asked what happens to a call at midnight in the first fifteen minutes of the first conversation.

Case 03 — Cleaning, Nashville

Nashville Steam Cleaning

Residential rounds and commercial contracts, booked off the phone.

Contracts a month, baseline12
Contracts a month, month 527
Rounds still on at 6 months24 of 27
PeriodFive months

The situation

One campaign covering everything from a single oven clean to a recurring office contract, with the same bids on both.

What was actually broken

Recurring work, worth thousands a year, was competing for budget with two-hundred-dollar one-offs. Nobody could say what a booked round cost to win.

What we changed

Split recurring and one-off into separate campaigns with separate budgets, capped the one-offs, and moved commercial onto business hours with its own page.

What else was happening

They took on two extra cleaners in March, which is part of why the round count could grow at all. Without the hire this is a smaller number.

What we would do differently

We should have asked for their retention figures in week one. It turned out the one-off customers almost never came back, which made the split obvious.

Case 04 — Mould and damp specialist

Four flat months, and a refunded invoice

Name withheld at their request.

Relevant searches in their market~400 a month
Booked jobs after four monthsFlat
Monthly fee$750
Last invoiceRefunded

The situation

A mould and damp specialist in a market with roughly 400 relevant searches a month. We had checked that number before we quoted, and took the job anyway.

What was actually broken

Nothing in the account, in the end. There was simply not enough demand for our fee to pay for itself, however well the campaigns were built.

What we changed

Tighter match types, a proper inspection page, bids off the searches that were only ever going to be homeowners looking for advice. It moved the cost per lead and not the job count.

What happened

Four flat months. We refunded the last invoice and told them to stop paying anybody, including us.

What we would do differently

We now add up searchable demand before we quote, and if our fee needs more extra booked work than the market can produce, we say no. We have turned down three companies since.
