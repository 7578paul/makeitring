The problems on a restoration account

Four things owners say on the first call, and what we do about each.

01

The 2am water call, and whether anyone picks it up

“Half my work comes in overnight and at weekends. If I am on a job I miss it, and by the time I ring back at eight they have had someone in.”

What owners tell us

We count what happens to your calls by hour before we touch a single bid.

Call recording goes on first. Then bids follow your real answer rate: up between 6pm and 2am when someone answers, hard down in the hours the recordings show voicemail. On one account, 23:00 to 06:00 was 31% of spend and 6% of booked jobs until an answering service went on. We will tell you what those cost and which ones clients use, and take no commission on either.

02

Emergency, mitigation and mold in one campaign

“It is all water damage as far as Google is concerned, isn't it.”

What owners tell us

It is not, and in one campaign the survey clicks quietly eat the emergency budget by Wednesday afternoon.

Burst pipe flooded kitchen now and mold survey cost are different jobs, different money and different urgency. We split emergency extraction, mitigation and drying, fire and smoke, and mold into their own campaigns, pages and budgets. Fire buyers ask about odour and contents. Mold buyers ask about health and landlords. One of those four is not even paid for by the homeowner.

03

Storm weeks, and the quiet months that pay for them

“One storm and we are turning work away for two weeks. Then May comes and we are chasing our tails.”

What owners tell us

Budgets go up the day before a warning, not the day after.

Click prices jump within hours of a warning being issued, and the firm already bidding takes the first calls. When every crew is committed we turn spend down rather than pay peak prices for a backlog, because a flooded kitchen will not wait nine days for you. In the quiet months the money moves to work that books ahead: mold and damp surveys, insurance reinstatement, contents cleaning, landlord and property-manager work.

04

Searches that can never become a job

“Some of my best work comes through two adjusters, and Google keeps sending me people who want to hire a dehumidifier.”

What owners tell us

Restoration search is full of equipment hire, DIY drying, claims advice, students and people looking for a job with you.

On accounts we have taken over, 19% to 34% of ninety-day spend went to queries that could never become work. Clearing it is a weekly grind through the search terms report, not a one-time setup. Separately, we do not pretend ads replace adjuster relationships: where insurance work is most of your revenue, the audit says so and sizes the budget to fill the gaps around it.
